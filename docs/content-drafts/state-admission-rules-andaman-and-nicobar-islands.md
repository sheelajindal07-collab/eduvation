## 4. Domicile / state-quota basics for admission to A&N government institutions

| Fact | Value | Source | Access date | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No single generic "domicile certificate" — a five-tier category system instead | Andaman & Nicobar Islands does not use one generic "domicile/resident" certificate for admission quotas. Instead, both DBRAIT (engineering) and ANIIMS (medical) apply the **same** five-category islands-residency scheme, each requiring its own specific certificate: **Category I — Tribals of A&N Islands** (certificate signed by the Tehsildar of the jurisdiction); **Category II — Deputationists and Central Government employees with transfer liability to serve outside the UT**, provided the candidate studied the last two years in the islands and passed the qualifying exam from a school in the islands (certificate signed by the competent departmental authority); **Category III — Settlers who were settled prior to 1942, and those settled under rehabilitation schemes introduced after reoccupation of the islands** (Tehsildar-signed certificate; seats split 1/3 pre-1942 settlers, 1/3 other settlers, 1/3 combined merit of the two); **Category IV — Other locals** not falling under I-III, or Central Government employees with **no** transfer liability, provided all have had 10 years' education in the islands (Tehsildar/departmental certificate); **Category V — General merit quota**, open to all A&N residents regardless of the above classifications, provided they studied the last 2 years in the islands and passed the qualifying exam from a school in the islands (supported by the Class XI/XII certificate itself) — this category also absorbs any seats left unfilled by Categories I–IV. | DBRAIT Admission Prospectus 2026-27 and ANIIMS MBBS Admission Prospectus 2026-27 (both give near-identical wording for these five categories) — both read verbatim via `pdftotext`: https://collegeadmission.andamannicobar.gov.in/images/dbrait.pdf and https://collegeadmission.andamannicobar.gov.in/images/aniims.pdf | 2026-09-21 | High — quoted/paraphrased directly from two independent official prospectuses that state the categories in near-identical terms, which cross-corroborates them |
| Statutory basis for the category scheme | The category percentages for ANIIMS's 87 state-quota seats are explicitly tied to "the distribution scheme notified by Ministry of Home Affairs, Govt. of India, vide order no: V-14040/5/96-ANL dated 30th May, 1996." DBRAIT's prospectus calls its version "Admission Criteria: Andaman & Nicobar Administration Guidelines" without separately citing the same 1996 order number, but the category definitions and percentages are functionally identical, strongly suggesting a common origin. A verifier should confirm whether DBRAIT admissions are formally governed by the same 1996 MHA order or a separate but parallel A&N Administration order. | Same two prospectus PDFs | 2026-09-21 | High for the ANIIMS citation (quoted directly); Medium for the inference that DBRAIT runs on the same 1996 order, since DBRAIT's own document does not cite that order number itself |
| Central (non-islands-specific) overlay reservations | 10% Economically Weaker Section (EWS) seats — proof required is an Income & Asset Certificate from a competent authority (family income and asset criteria per the central EWS scheme, e.g. under Rs. 8 lakh/year at DBRAIT) — and, for technical/engineering diploma and degree seats specifically, a further 5% Tuition Fee Waiver (TFW) scheme (family income under Rs. 6 lakh/year) — both supernumerary to the approved intake, under central AICTE/UGC/DoPT-style rules rather than being specific to A&N | Same two prospectus PDFs, read verbatim | 2026-09-21 | High — quoted directly |
| Unfilled-seat cascade rule | Per ANIIMS's prospectus (Section 4.2, quoted): "the unutilized seats of Cat-I, II, III & IV will directly go to Cat-V ... in the event of any unutilized seats of AIQ or EWS quota, the said seats shall be allotted to Cat-I, II, III & IV as per the reservation scheme criteria of Islands and thereafter if the seats remain unutilized, they will go to Cat-V." | ANIIMS MBBS Admission Prospectus 2026-27, read verbatim | 2026-09-21 | High — quoted directly |
| Small-UT comparison note (context, not a finding of "no infrastructure") | Unlike some small states/UTs that have little or no admissions infrastructure of their own, Andaman & Nicobar Islands **does** run its own distinctive admissions machinery: a UT-specific category-based residency/quota scheme (not found replicated elsewhere in the states researched so far in this pipeline), its own degree-granting umbrella university (NSCBIHL) for its constituent colleges, and a single shared online admission portal (CCAP) covering all 14 of its higher-education institutions, including its sole engineering college (DBRAIT) and sole medical college (ANIIMS). What this file did **not** confirm is the exact "home state" treatment A&N candidates receive when seeking seats at mainland NITs via JoSAA (Section 2) and the precise title of the body running UT-quota MBBS counselling day-to-day (Section 3) — both flagged above as genuine "not found" gaps, not evidence that no infrastructure exists. | Synthesis of Sections 1-4 above | 2026-09-21 | High confidence that the category scheme, NSCBIHL and CCAP are genuine, well-corroborated A&N-specific infrastructure (all read verbatim from primary sources above); the two narrower gaps are honestly reported as "not found" |
